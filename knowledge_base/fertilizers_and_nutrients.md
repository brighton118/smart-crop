# Fertilizers and Nutrient Management

## Essential Macronutrients
- **Nitrogen (N)**: Crucial for leaf and stem growth. Deficiency shows as pale green or yellowing older leaves. Sources: Urea, CAN, Compost, Cow Dung.
- **Phosphorus (P)**: Essential for root development and flowering. Deficiency shows as dark green or purplish leaves and stunted growth. Sources: DAP, SSP, Rock Phosphate, Poultry Manure.
- **Potassium (K)**: Vital for fruit quality and disease resistance. Deficiency shows as browning/scorching at the leaf edges. Sources: MOP, NPK blends, Wood Ash, Banana peels.

## Organic vs. Inorganic Fertilizers
### Organic Fertilizers (Manure, Compost, Bio-slurry)
- **Advantages**: Improves soil structure (water retention and aeration), encourages beneficial soil microbes, effects last for years.
- **Application**: Must be well-decomposed before application. Too much fresh manure can "burn" plants because it acts hot during decomposition.
- **Best Practice for Smallholders**: Integration of livestock (cows, goats, pigs) with crop production strictly to harvest manure.

### Inorganic Fertilizers (NPK 17:17:17, DAP 18:46:0, Urea 46:0:0)
- **Advantages**: Immediate release of nutrients, accurate dosage, high concentrations.
- **Disadvantages**: Costly for smallholders, does not improve soil structure, and can make soils acidic over time if used without liming or organic matter.

## Fertilizer Application Methods
- **Broadcasting**: Scattering fertilizer over the field. Often wasteful and causes heavy weed growth.
- **Banding / Ring Application**: Standard for bananas and coffee. Digging a shallow ring around the plant base (at the drip line, where the roots actively feed) and burying the fertilizer.
- **Top Dressing**: Applying nitrogen fertilizers (like Urea) after the crop is already growing and established (e.g., knee-high maize).
