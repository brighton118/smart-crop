# Post-Harvest Handling and Climate-Smart Agriculture

## Post-Harvest Handling
Post-harvest losses in Sub-Saharan Africa claim up to 30% of total production. Proper handling is critical to maximize farm income.

### Grains and Cereals (Maize, Beans)
- **Drying**: Grain must be dried to 13-14% moisture content before storage to prevent Aflatoxin contamination (a highly toxic fungus).
- **Tarpaulins vs Bare Earth**: Never dry grain on the bare ground. Use clean tarpaulins, raised drying racks, or solar dryers.
- **Storage**: Use hermetic (airtight) bags like PICS bags. When sealed perfectly, any weevils inside consume the oxygen and die, preventing infestation without the use of chemical dusts.

### Fresh Produce (Tomatoes, Vegetables)
- **Harvesting**: Pluck early in the morning or late evening. Avoid harvesting during the hot mid-day sun.
- **Handling**: Use rigid plastic crates instead of traditional woven baskets or sacks which crush the produce at the bottom.
- **Storage**: Keep in shaded, well-ventilated areas, or use a Charcoal Cooler (a zero-energy cooling chamber) to extend shelf life.

## Climate-Smart Agriculture (CSA)
CSA is an approach that helps guide actions specifically to transform and reorient agricultural systems to effectively support development and ensure food security in a changing climate.

### Three Pillars of CSA:
1. **Productivity**: Sustainably increasing agricultural productivity and incomes.
2. **Adaptation**: Building resilience to climate change (e.g., drought-resistant seeds, water harvesting, smart irrigation automation).
3. **Mitigation**: Reducing greenhouse gas emissions (e.g., zero-tillage, avoiding bush burning, biogas digesters managing livestock waste).

### Practical CSA Technologies in East Africa:
- Use of Zai pits (planting holes filled with organic matter to trap rainwater) in semi-arid zones.
- Agroforestry (growing trees alongside crops).
- Minimum tillage / Conservation agriculture to prevent soil moisture loss.
