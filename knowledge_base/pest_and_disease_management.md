# Pest and Disease Management

## Common Pests in East Africa
- **Fall Armyworm (FAW)**: A devastating pest for maize. FAW feeds on the leaves and stalks, deeply reducing yield. 
  - *Management*: Early planting to avoid peak pest populations. Use of neem-based biopesticides. Push-pull technology (planting Desmodium between maize rows and Napier grass around the field).
- **Banana Weevil (Kiwotoka)**: Burrows into the base of the banana pseudostem, causing the plant to snap.
  - *Management*: Clean planting materials, rigorous pseudostem trapping, and maintaining a clean plantation.
- **Coffee Twig Borer (BCTB)**: Burrows into robusta coffee branches causing them to dry up and die.
  - *Management*: Prune out and burn the affected twigs. Maintain proper shade and avoid intercropping with alternative host plants like some shade trees.

## Common Diseases
- **Banana Bacterial Wilt (BBW)**: Identified by yellowing leaves, premature ripening, and foul-smelling yellow pus inside the pseudostem.
  - *Control*: Strict field hygiene. Disinfect tools using fire or Jik (bleach). Remove and bury infected mats completely. Cut male buds early using a forked stick.
- **Coffee Wilt Disease (CWD)**: Causes defoliation and death of robusta coffee. 
  - *Control*: Uprooting and burning affected plants. Using CWD-resistant varieties distributed by UCDA (Uganda Coffee Development Authority).
- **Tomato Blight (Late and Early)**: Very common during the rainy season.
  - *Control*: Copper-based fungicides. Crop rotation. High pruning to prevent leaves from touching the soil.

## Integrated Pest Management (IPM)
IPM is a strategy that focuses on long-term prevention of pests through a combination of techniques:
1. Biological control (e.g., using ladybugs to eat aphids).
2. Habitat manipulation (e.g., Push-pull technology).
3. Modification of cultural practices (e.g., crop rotation, early planting).
4. Use of resistant varieties.
Chemical pesticides are used only as a last resort.
