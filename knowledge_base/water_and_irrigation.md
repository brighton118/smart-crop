# Water and Irrigation Practices

## Rain-Fed vs. Irrigated Agriculture
Traditionally, agriculture in Uganda relies heavily on bi-modal rainfall patterns (two rainy seasons: March-May and September-November). However, climate change has caused irregularities, making irrigation increasingly critical for food security and commercial farming.

## Irrigation Methods
### 1. Drip Irrigation
Highly recommended for vegetable farming (tomatoes, cabbages, onions) and high-value crops. 
- **Advantages**: Water efficiency (up to 90%), reduced weed growth, and the ability to apply fertilizer through the water (fertigation).
- **Setup for Smallholders**: Gravity-fed drip kits using 200-litre elevated drums are popular and cost-effective.

### 2. Sprinkler Irrigation
Used for robust crops or pastures.
- **Disadvantages**: High evaporation rates under the hot sun, high energy requirement for pumping, and can encourage fungal diseases if leaves remain wet.

### 3. Basin / Flood Irrigation
Most commonly used in rice paddies in eastern Uganda (Doho, Kibimba). Extremely water-intensive and requires flat terrain.

## Smart Irrigation & Sensor-Based Automation
Modern farms are beginning to deploy IoT (Internet of Things) moisture sensors to measure Volumetric Water Content (VWC) in real-time.
- **Automation threshold**: Systems typically trigger irrigation when moisture drops below 25-30%.
- **Benefits**: Prevents overwatering (which causes root rot in crops like beans and coffee), drastically reduces water pumping costs, and maximizes yield.
