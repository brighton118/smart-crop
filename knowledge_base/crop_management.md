# Crop Management and Good Agricultural Practices

## Staple Crops of Uganda

### Bananas (Matooke)
- **Environment**: Thrives in deep, well-drained soils (Nitisols) with high organic matter.
- **Spacing**: Usually 3m x 3m. 
- **Management**: Regular de-suckering (keeping only 3 plants per mat: mother, daughter, and granddaughter) and mulching are critical to retain moisture and soil fertility.

### Coffee (Robusta and Arabica)
- **Robusta**: Grown around the Lake Victoria basin and central Uganda at lower altitudes.
- **Arabica**: Grown in high-altitude areas (Mount Elgon, Rwenzori mountains).
- **Pruning**: Essential to remove dead wood and encourage new productive branches. Stumping is done every 5-7 years to rejuvenate old trees.
- **Shade Trees**: Planting shade trees (like Ficus or Albizia) improves coffee quality, provides drought resistance, and adds leaf litter to the soil.

### Maize
- **Planting**: Best planted at the onset of rains. Spacing is generally 75cm between rows and 30cm between plants.
- **Weeding**: Crucial during the first 4 weeks to avoid competition for nutrients.
- **Drought Resilience**: Sacha or Longe varieties are bred to withstand dry spells better than traditional seeds.

## Crop Rotation
Always rotate crops belonging to different families to break pest and disease cycles. For example, never plant tomatoes directly after potatoes (both are Solanaceae). A good rotation rule is: Legume -> Root Crop -> Cereal.
